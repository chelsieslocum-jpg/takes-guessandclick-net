      About Takes

      Where your recordings go

      Takes never uploads anything. You choose where your files go.

      The detail

      Captions and trimmed export download their tools from free public download servers when you use them. They may download again each time you open the page. The size is shown before the download starts.

      Those downloads come from cdn.jsdelivr.net and huggingface.co, which hands the caption model off to its own delivery host, us.aws.cdn.hf.co. Nothing else is contacted.

      Saving to a cloud folder writes the file into a folder you pick. Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox or iCloud does any uploading from there.

      What it saves

      Loom Business is $18 per user per month, and Business + AI is $24, at monthly billing. That is $216 to $288 a year for one person. Takes is free.

      Prices from Loom's own pricing page, checked October 3rd, 2026.

      Good to know

      • Works in current Chrome and Edge.
      • Captions are English only.
      • Trimming the start and end, and cutting a section from the middle, both change the saved file. Saving an edited recording takes a few seconds per minute of video.
      • Captions show on the video here and save as a separate .vtt file. To draw them into the video too, tick "Burn captions into the video" under Save options.
      • The camera bubble can show a video file you choose in place of your live camera.
      • The camera bubble can be a circle or a rectangle.
      • The transcript can be searched, copied and saved as text or subtitles.
      • When you save, you can burn the captions into the video and choose a tall 9:16 shape for social posts.
      • The cloud folder gives you a file. You make the share link in Google Drive, OneDrive, Dropbox or iCloud.
      • On a Mac, the system asks for screen recording permission the first time, and Chrome needs a restart after you allow it.
      • Download anything you want to keep. Clearing your browser data removes your Library.
      • Only record what you have the right to record.
